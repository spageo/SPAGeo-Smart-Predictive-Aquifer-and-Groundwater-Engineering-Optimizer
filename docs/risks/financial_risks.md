# SPAGeo Financial Risks

## Purpose

This document defines the financial risks identified for the SPAGeo project and the planned mitigation measures associated with funding availability, revenue generation, and broader economic conditions.

The probability and impact ratings in this document are planning assessments. They should be reviewed and updated as funding requirements, revenue performance, operating costs, and market conditions become better understood.

## Financial Risk Summary

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Insufficient funding | Medium | High | Phased development, grants |
| Revenue model failure | Medium | High | Multiple revenue streams |
| Economic downturn | Low | Medium | Essential software for water mgmt |

## 1. Insufficient Funding

**Probability:** Medium

**Impact:** High

**Risk:** Available funding may be insufficient to support planned product development, infrastructure, staffing, commercialization, and other operating requirements within the intended development timeline.

**Mitigation:** The supplied strategy identifies phased development and grants as mitigation measures. Phased development can prioritize critical capabilities and manage expenditure according to available resources, while grants can provide additional non-dilutive funding opportunities where applicable.

**Monitoring considerations:**

- Track available cash and committed funding.
- Monitor development expenditure against approved plans.
- Review funding requirements at each development phase.
- Identify relevant grant and funding opportunities.
- Prioritize product milestones according to available resources.
- Maintain contingency planning for funding delays.

## 2. Revenue Model Failure

**Probability:** Medium

**Impact:** High

**Risk:** The planned revenue model may not generate sufficient recurring or commercial revenue to support ongoing development and operations.

Potential causes include lower-than-expected customer conversion, pricing challenges, longer enterprise sales cycles, limited willingness to pay, or changes in customer requirements.

**Mitigation:** The supplied strategy identifies multiple revenue streams as the primary mitigation measure. A diversified commercial model can reduce dependence on a single source of revenue and allow the project to adapt offerings to different customer segments and use cases.

**Monitoring considerations:**

- Track revenue against projections.
- Monitor free-to-paid conversion rates.
- Monitor Pro and Enterprise customer acquisition.
- Track average revenue per customer.
- Review pricing and packaging based on market feedback.
- Monitor recurring revenue and customer retention.
- Evaluate the performance of each revenue stream independently.

## 3. Economic Downturn

**Probability:** Low

**Impact:** Medium

**Risk:** A broader economic downturn may reduce software budgets, delay procurement decisions, slow customer acquisition, or affect investment availability.

**Mitigation:** The supplied strategy identifies the essential nature of water-management software as a potential mitigation factor. Maintaining focus on practical groundwater and water-management workflows can support the product's relevance to organizations that need to manage critical water resources.

**Monitoring considerations:**

- Monitor economic conditions in target markets.
- Track changes in customer software and technology budgets.
- Monitor procurement delays and sales-cycle length.
- Review investment and funding-market conditions.
- Prioritize capabilities that address essential water-management workflows.
- Maintain cost discipline during periods of weaker demand.

## Financial Risk Management Approach

Financial risks should be reviewed throughout product development, commercialization, and market expansion.

The project should periodically review:

- Funding availability and runway
- Development and operating costs
- Revenue performance
- Customer acquisition and conversion
- Recurring revenue and retention
- Performance of individual revenue streams
- Pricing and packaging
- Grant and funding opportunities
- Economic and procurement conditions
- Effectiveness of current mitigation measures

Risk status should be updated when material changes occur in funding availability, revenue performance, operating costs, pricing strategy, customer demand, or broader economic conditions.

## Key Principle

The financial-risk strategy is to control expenditure through phased development, diversify funding and revenue sources, monitor financial performance continuously, and preserve focus on practical water-management capabilities that support long-term product relevance.
